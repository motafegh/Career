**Owner:** Ali Rajabi  
**Status:** Approved and controlling employability and market-transition plan  
**Activated:** 2026-07-27  
**Responsibility:** Role calibration, requirement-to-evidence mapping, portfolio readiness, professional positioning, application activation, interview feedback, and market-driven strategy correction

## 1. Purpose

The Career system must convert project and learning work into a realistic path to technical employment.

The market plan connects:

```text
Career profile
→ target role families
→ representative current requirements
→ capability and evidence map
→ portfolio and positioning
→ application-readiness decision
→ applications and interviews
→ feedback patterns
→ strategy correction
→ first technical role
```

This plan does not assume that Ali must become fully independent in every listed technology before applying.

## 3. Market-evidence method

A market conclusion must use a representative sample rather than one memorable job description.

For each active role family:

1. collect a bounded sample of current public job descriptions across relevant locations and work modes;
2. separate common requirements from employer-specific preferences;
3. classify requirements as core, frequent, occasional, or exceptional;
4. identify experience-level expectations and hidden responsibility signals;
5. map each requirement to current evidence, partial evidence, or a gap;
6. distinguish true blockers from learnable-on-job requirements;
7. record geography, work mode, language, visa, compensation, and seniority constraints only from current verified information;
8. review patterns, not isolated listings.

Current market facts must be refreshed when decisions depend on them.

## 4. Requirement-to-evidence map

Use this structure:

| Role responsibility | Market frequency | Required depth | Ali evidence | Assistance/transfer limit | Gap type | Career action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Gap types:

- **Blocking:** application would be implausible without evidence or positioning correction.
- **Material:** commonly required and worth addressing soon.
- **Differentiating:** not always required but improves competitiveness.
- **Learnable on job:** reasonable to apply without full evidence.
- **Employer-specific:** should not reshape the whole Career plan.
- **Unknown:** requires more market or capability evidence.

## 7. Employability stages

### E0 — Direction defined

Required:

- honest profile;
- target role families;
- project portfolio roles;
- capability evidence model;
- geographic/work-mode direction.

### E1 — Market calibrated

Required:

- representative current job-description sample;
- common responsibility map;
- evidence and gap map;
- initial role prioritization;
- identification of true application blockers.

### E2 — Portfolio and positioning ready

Required:

- resume draft with evidence-bounded claims;
- GitHub/profile review;
- at least one explainable flagship artifact;
- historical-project claims where useful;
- concise project and capability narratives;
- known assistance and non-claims;
- practical contact and application materials handled privately.

### E3 — Application ready

Required:

- selected role bands and locations;
- no unresolved critical claim or privacy problem;
- ability to explain the flagship problem, flow, evidence, and Ali-owned work;
- preparation for common Python/data/debugging questions at the target level;
- application and feedback method;
- explicit decision that current evidence is sufficient to begin calibration applications.

E3 does not require every gap to be closed.

### E4 — Active market feedback

Required:

- a bounded application cadence;
- private or public-safe aggregate tracking;
- interview preparation tied to actual roles;
- classification of rejection, silence, screening, technical, and fit feedback;
- evidence-based corrections.

### E5 — Transition decision

Evaluate:

- offers or serious opportunities;
- role responsibility and mentorship;
- compensation and location privately;
- growth path;
- evidence alignment;
- whether the role advances the long-term direction.

## 8. Application-readiness decision

A formal readiness review should answer:

1. Which exact role families are active?
2. Which current evidence is strongest?
3. Which claims can be defended live?
4. Which gaps are blocking versus acceptable?
5. Is at least one project understandable to a reviewer?
6. Can Ali perform representative practical work with the stated assistance level?
7. Are resume, GitHub, privacy, and contact materials ready?
8. Which roles are realistic calibration applications?
9. What feedback will trigger correction?
10. What application cadence is sustainable?

Decision options:

- not ready; name exact blockers and reassessment;
- ready for calibration applications;
- ready for the primary target band;
- ready for a narrower role band;
- ready only through internships, trainee roles, contributions, or structured programs.

## 9. Portfolio requirements

The market-facing portfolio should prioritize:

- one flagship with a real user and decision;

- Ali's specific contribution;

## 10. Resume and GitHub positioning

A future resume should:

- lead with the current technical direction and strongest evidence;
- represent family-business experience as transferable professional responsibility;
- avoid invented employment titles or technical experience;
- use project bullets describing behavior, evidence, and Ali's role;
- avoid unsupported metrics;
- distinguish production-oriented work from production employment;
- include only skills supported by meaningful exposure or evidence;
- remain tailored to a role family rather than one universal resume.

GitHub positioning should:

- make the active flagship easy to identify;
- remove or contextualize misleading repository descriptions;
- provide concise project status and limitations;
- avoid presenting all old repositories as active or equally owned;
- show source, tests, evidence, and explanation rather than document count alone.

## 11. Interview preparation

Interview preparation activates after role patterns and readiness are established.

It should cover:

- Python reasoning and modification;
- testing and debugging;
- data and SQL where relevant;
- APIs and external-source failure;
- Git and collaboration;
- project architecture and trade-offs;
- AI-assisted work disclosure;
- practical exercises at the target level;
- behavioral examples from both business and technical work.

Do not memorize explanations without changed-case practice.

## 12. Feedback loop

Classify market feedback carefully:

- no response;
- recruiter screen failure;
- location/visa mismatch;
- experience mismatch;
- resume/positioning weakness;
- technical knowledge gap;
- practical implementation gap;
- communication gap;
- role-fit mismatch;
- compensation or availability mismatch;
- unknown.

One outcome does not justify a strategy change. Repeated or strong evidence may change role targeting, portfolio work, or capability priorities.

## 13. Privacy

Detailed applications, contact information, compensation, recruiter correspondence, interview material, and employer feedback should be private by default.

Career may retain public-safe aggregate conclusions and links to public job descriptions.

- application dossiers;

JobHunter does not automatically own or change:

- Career strategy;
- Ali capability claims;

- master-CV claims;

Its own implementation is not automatically Ali capability evidence. Market conclusions must remain sample-aware, source-aware, provenance-preserving, and reviewable.

Career should not copy JobHunter's database or detailed job corpus into this public repository. Prefer versioned Career-facing JobHunter artifacts and record only durable Career conclusions.
