## 1. Why this extension exists

The first A3 sample emphasized UAE/Dubai, Germany/Europe, and a small Singapore comparison. That was insufficient for Ali's actual Career reality because the Iranian/Persian employment market is also materially relevant and has its own hiring language, platforms, role taxonomy, compensation context, and entry-access pattern.

This extension asks:

1. Is there current Iranian demand for the applied-AI / AI-engineering / agent / automation responsibilities identified in A1–A2?
2. Does the Iranian market favor the same anchored-hybrid shape found internationally, or a materially different profile?
3. What genuinely entry-level routes are visible versus specialist/mid-level roles?
4. Which conventional engineering substrates still appear beside AI work?
5. How trustworthy are headline AI-job counts on the major Persian job platforms?
6. What should the later Career review infer — and not infer — from the Iran market?

This document extends A3 only. It does not decide Ali's application geography, salary target, capability level, working identity, or whether applications should activate.

## 2. Sources and sampling method

Primary requested Persian-market sources:

- Jobinja — `https://jobinja.ir/`
- JobVision — `https://jobvision.ir/jobs`
- e-estekhdam — `https://www.e-estekhdam.com/`
- IranTalent — `https://www.irantalent.com/jobs`

Supplementary indexed mirrors/search results were used only when a platform's dynamic job page did not expose the individual posting text reliably to the research tools. Such results are treated as weaker evidence than the original employer/platform page.

### Evidence-strength rule

Use the strongest claim each source permits:

```text
full current job description
→ responsibility / requirement evidence

current platform/indexed title only
→ existence / taxonomy evidence only

platform keyword result count
→ search-surface evidence only

salary report
→ compensation-context evidence for its exact level/category only
```

No population-level prevalence percentage is inferred from these targeted searches.

## 3. Platform quality and interpretation limits

### 3.1 Jobinja

Jobinja is a large general Iranian job marketplace. Its home page recently exposed roughly 14,881 active advertisements across roughly 6,283 Iranian companies, which makes it useful for broad-market discovery.

A targeted A3 search for `هوش مصنوعی` produced hundreds of matching opportunities. The result set mixed genuine engineering roles with content creation, design, teaching, video/media, marketing, and other roles that merely use AI.

Examples visible during the targeted search included titles such as:

- `مهندس هوش مصنوعی گفتار` — Speech AI Engineer;
- `مهندس هوش مصنوعی` — AI Engineer;
- `متخصص هوش مصنوعی` — AI Specialist;
- remote AI Specialist;
- AI semiconductor specialist;
- Senior AI/ML Engineer;
- alongside AI-content/design/teaching roles.

**Calibration:** Jobinja is strong evidence that AI has spread widely through Iranian job taxonomy, but a raw `هوش مصنوعی` result count is a poor proxy for AI-engineering demand. Responsibility-level filtering is mandatory.

The individual Jobinja descriptions were not consistently recoverable during this research pass, so these examples are used primarily as current title/existence evidence rather than detailed requirement evidence.

## 4. JobVision: explicit AI/LLM/agent specialization is visible

JobVision's current/recent indexed surface showed a notably specific AI-engineering vocabulary rather than only generic `کارشناس هوش مصنوعی` titles.

Examples found during this A3 extension included:

- `کارشناس هوش مصنوعی و علوم داده`;
- `مهندس هوش مصنوعی`;
- `AI Engineer (Mid-Level | LLM FastAPI)`;
- `متخصص هوش مصنوعی (AI Specialist / LLM Engineer)`;
- `کارشناس هوش مصنوعی و علم داده (اتوماسیون هوشمندسازی)`;
- `کارشناس علم داده و هوش مصنوعی`;
- `کارشناس هوش مصنوعی و ایجنت`;
- `کارشناس اتوماسیون هوش مصنوعی (استقرار و اجرا)`;
- `برنامه‌نویس هوش مصنوعی (AI)`;
- `توسعه‌دهنده چت‌بات (هوش مصنوعی)`;
- Speech/AI engineering roles.

A recent Rasha Holding cluster is particularly useful taxonomy evidence because the same employer exposed separate roles for:

```text
data science + AI
AI + agents
AI specialization
AI-assisted content work
```

That separation itself matters: it shows that at least some Iranian employers now distinguish **engineering/agent responsibilities** from merely using generative AI for content.

### Entry evidence

A current/recent `کارآموز هوش مصنوعی` posting from Rasha was also surfaced during the sample. A `Junior AI Engineer` title from earlier in 2026 provides additional supporting evidence that the domestic market is not exclusively senior-level.

However, many of the visible current engineering titles are `متخصص`, `کارشناس`, or explicitly mid-level. Therefore this sample does **not** justify saying that Iran has a broad or easy junior-AI market.

**Calibration:** entry routes exist, but much of the visible AI-engineering demand still expects specialist-level or professional responsibility.

## 5. IranTalent: AI is a formal occupational category, not only a trend keyword

IranTalent / IranSalary provides stronger structured context than simple keyword search.

Its 1405 salary taxonomy includes `علوم داده و هوش مصنوعی` as a formal category and explicitly maps titles such as:

- Data Scientist;
- AI Specialist / AI Engineer;
- Machine Learning Engineer;
- NLP Specialist / NLP Engineer;
- Computer Vision Engineer.

The same report notes that newer positions may include generative AI and large language models, with responsibility ranging from simple API use to architecture and evaluation of more complex systems.

Current/recent related opportunities shown by IranTalent included:

- `توسعه‌دهنده AI (Vibe Coder)`;
- `مهندس نرم‌ افزار و توسعه‌ دهنده هوش مصنوعی`;
- AI project-management and AI-adjacent roles.

### Why the `Vibe Coder` title matters — and what it does not prove

The presence of an explicit `Vibe Coder` job title is useful evidence that AI-assisted implementation language has entered Iranian hiring vocabulary.

It does **not** establish that conventional software competence is obsolete, that this title is common, or that other employers accept unverified agent output. One title is taxonomy evidence, not a market-wide ownership standard.

## 6. Conventional engineering substrate remains visible in Iran

The Iranian evidence does not support replacing software foundations with prompting or agent use.

IranTalent's 1405 junior Python/Django material frames junior backend competence around responsibilities such as:

- API/backend work;
- database/query work;
- writing tests;
- Git and code review;
- Linux/server exposure;
- mentorship/team growth.

A current related Python Developer opportunity was also present in that search surface.

On the data side, Iranian data-engineering descriptions continue to involve SQL/T-SQL, data modeling/warehouse/ETL responsibilities, pipeline tooling, and in some roles Docker/Kubernetes or orchestration technologies.

Meanwhile, current AI titles increasingly combine AI with:

```text
software development
+ FastAPI/backend
+ data science/data handling
+ automation/deployment
+ LLMs
+ agents
+ domain-specific systems such as speech
```

**Calibration:** the Iranian market is consistent with the international A3 finding that AI work is increasingly hybrid but still sits on an engineering/data substrate.

## 7. e-estekhdam: useful breadth signal, weaker technical-detail source in this pass

e-estekhdam is a broad national hiring source rather than a technology-specialist marketplace. Current indexed/channel evidence showed active AI hiring, including an advertisement for an AI specialist at Elite Engineering Group across 12 provinces.

This is useful evidence that AI hiring is not limited to a single Tehran technology-company niche.

However, the current research path did not recover enough fresh, detailed e-estekhdam technical descriptions to infer recurring Python/SQL/LLM/testing requirements from this source alone.

Older detailed AI postings on e-estekhdam have included data preparation, ML, deployment, software integration, programming languages and business-problem responsibilities, but those older descriptions are **not** counted as current-market frequency evidence.

**Calibration:** e-estekhdam contributes current existence/geographic-breadth evidence, but should carry less weight than detailed current descriptions when deciding technical priorities.

## 8. AI job taxonomy is noisier in the Persian market

One of the strongest Iran-specific findings is the breadth — and ambiguity — of the label `هوش مصنوعی`.

The same search ecosystems can include:

### Engineering / technical AI

- AI Engineer;
- LLM Engineer;
- AI + agent specialist;
- AI automation deployment;
- data science + AI;
- speech AI;
- AI software developer;
- chatbot/LLM development.

### AI-enabled non-engineering work

- AI content creation;
- AI-assisted design;
- AI video/media work;
- teaching/mentoring;
- marketing/content roles that use generative tools.

Therefore:

> `AI` in an Iranian job title is an especially weak capability signal unless the description shows engineering responsibility.

For Career and JobHunter use, future Persian-market acquisition should classify at least:

```text
AI engineering / software / data / agent role
AI-enabled non-engineering role
AI-adjacent management/product role
unclear from title
```

before computing role counts or skill recurrence.

## 9. Anchored-hybrid hypothesis: Iranian market result

The Iran/Persian extension **supports** rather than overturns the A2/A3 anchored-hybrid hypothesis.

Visible current Iranian titles and structured category evidence show combinations such as:

```text
Python/software engineering
+ data / ML
+ LLM / generative AI
+ agents / automation
+ backend/API frameworks
+ deployment/integration
+ domain-specific knowledge
```

This suggests that a bounded hybrid engineer who can move across software, data, AI systems and integration has plausible domestic-market relevance.

But this evidence also argues against shallow generalism. Titles such as LLM Engineer, Speech AI Engineer, AI/ML Engineer and AI + Agent Specialist imply that employers still value enough technical depth to own a particular responsibility class.

### Best current interpretation

```text
Iran market does not imply:
"learn every AI tool"

Iran market more plausibly implies:
"build a credible engineering substrate,
then combine it with AI/data/agent responsibilities
that solve actual product/business problems"
```

That is structurally consistent with the international sample.

## 10. Entry-access comparison with UAE / Europe

The Iranian market changes the **access problem**, even though it does not fundamentally change the technical direction.

### International sample commonly introduced gates such as

- work authorization / visa sponsorship;
- nationality-restricted graduate programs;
- campus/recent-graduate status;
- German-language requirements;
- country-specific residency programs.

### Iranian postings can avoid some of those international gates

For an Iranian candidate, local roles may not carry the same visa/work-authorization barrier. Current samples also show internship/junior pathways alongside specialist roles.

But that does **not** make the domestic market automatically easier. Different practical filters can matter, including:

- concentration of technology jobs in Tehran and a smaller set of major cities;
- employer-specific degree or experience expectations;
- company/team quality and access to strong technical mentorship;
- compensation and currency purchasing-power considerations;
- local on-site/hybrid expectations;
- employer-specific demographic or administrative requirements where explicitly stated.

These should be evaluated vacancy by vacancy if Iran becomes an active application geography. A3 does not generalize them into universal Iranian hiring rules.

## 11. Compensation context — useful but not directly comparable across levels

IranTalent's 1405 salary report for **Data Science / AI at `کارشناس` level in Tehran** reports approximately:

- median requested net compensation: **50 million toman/month**;
- middle 50%: approximately **37–71 million toman/month**;
- reported confidence for Tehran: high.

Its junior Python/Django salary material reports a lower junior-market benchmark.

These figures must **not** be converted into a claim that AI pays a fixed percentage more than Python because the categories are not level-matched: one is an `expert / کارشناس` AI/data category while the other is explicitly junior Python.

The useful conclusion is narrower:

> IranTalent has enough market data to treat Data Science / AI as a distinct professional compensation category, but salary decisions require matched seniority, city, company, and responsibility scope.

Salary should remain secondary to role quality, mentorship, technical responsibility, accessibility, and Career trajectory when evaluating Ali's first technical role.

## 12. Iran-specific role-family implications

### Strong domestic-market relevance

**Applied AI / AI Engineering / LLM-agent implementation**  
Clearly visible in current Persian job taxonomy. The market includes generic AI Engineer roles as well as LLM, agent, automation, speech and AI-software combinations.

**AI + software/backend integration**  
Titles combining AI with FastAPI/software development support the value of a Python/software anchor rather than an isolated ML identity.

**Data Science / AI hybrid**  
A formal IranTalent category and multiple current JobVision-style titles support this as a real market family, though the experience/depth requirements vary substantially.

**AI automation / deployment / implementation**  
Current titles explicitly mentioning automation, deployment and execution are especially relevant to Ali's interest in orchestration and AI-enabled engineering responsibility.

### Still important conventional bands

**Python/backend** and **data engineering** remain useful anchors because they supply the implementation, API, database, testing and deployment substrate that current AI roles build upon.

### Entry-level caution

The domestic market contains internships/junior examples, but visible current AI roles skew enough toward `کارشناس`, `متخصص`, and mid-level language that Career should not assume an easy direct entry into a broad AI-engineer title.

Portfolio-driven proof may help, but A4 must assess whether Ali can currently defend the required engineering responsibility.

## 13. What this extension changes in A3

The original A3 findings remain valid, with three additions:

1. **Iran should be treated as a distinct, potentially relevant market lane rather than omitted from Career calibration.**
2. **AI/LLM/agent engineering is visibly present in Persian hiring language**, including explicit agent, LLM, AI automation and AI-assisted coding terminology.
3. **Persian-market AI searches require stronger taxonomy filtering** than raw keyword/title counts because engineering and non-engineering `AI` roles are heavily mixed.

The extension does **not** justify changing Career identity or application activation by itself.

## 14. Combined A3 implication after adding Iran

Across UAE, Europe, Singapore-supporting evidence, and now Iran, the strongest common pattern is increasingly stable:

```text
credible software/programming substrate
+ APIs/integration
+ data/database competence
+ testing/debugging/evaluation
+ AI/LLM/agent leverage where role-relevant
+ ability to translate requirements into working systems
```

The markets differ more strongly in **access filters, language, credential rules, compensation, and exact role taxonomy** than in the underlying direction of engineering responsibility.

This makes the A2 anchored-hybrid model more credible, but A4 remains necessary because market fit cannot be inferred from repository sophistication or from AI tool use.

## 15. What the evidence does not justify

Do not infer from this extension that:

- hundreds of Jobinja `هوش مصنوعی` matches equal hundreds of AI-engineering jobs;
- `Vibe Coder` is a common or accepted universal Iranian engineering title;
- Iranian AI roles no longer require programming fundamentals;
- every AI role requires LLM/agent expertise;
- Iran is automatically easier or better than UAE/Europe for Ali;
- the reported IranTalent AI salary is a junior benchmark;
- Ali is currently qualified for the observed AI/LLM/agent roles;
- Career should activate Iran applications before A4/A7.

## 16. Recommended future Persian-market sampling discipline

If Iran becomes an active application lane, use the four requested platforms as complementary sources rather than interchangeable ones:

### Jobinja
Best for broad company/job discovery. Filter aggressively by actual engineering responsibility.

### JobVision
Useful for current technical titles and AI/data/agent role discovery. Preserve employer description where available and distinguish junior/mid/specialist levels.

### IranTalent
Useful for structured professional categories, salary context, and more conventional corporate role framing. Match salary comparisons by level.

### e-estekhdam
Useful for national breadth and opportunities outside the narrow technology-company ecosystem. Require full description before deriving technical skill frequency.

Future JobHunter integration should preserve platform/source identity and avoid merging their counts as though they were one neutral sample.
