# Career Operating Contract

**Owner:** Ali Rajabi  
**Status:** Approved and controlling Career operating contract  
**Activated:** 2026-07-27  
**Responsibility:** Workload, review cadence, project allocation, change control, anti-diversion behavior, evidence discipline, and proportional Career operation

## 1. Boundary

This contract governs career-level operation.

It does not govern ordinary UpgradePilot or other project implementation, technical plans, exact next actions, commands, source changes, tests, blockers, working memory, learning sessions, or project-local execution methods.

Project repositories own project operation. Career inspects them only when a career question, review, capability assessment, portfolio claim, workload decision, strategy decision, or Career-defined evidence gate requires it.

### Career-to-project handoff

Career may decide **what career outcome or evidence is required**, including:

- capability or ownership evidence that must exist before a stronger claim;
- portfolio or CV evidence/claim boundaries;
- market or application-readiness requirements;
- project role and allocation;
- workload ceilings;
- acceptance conditions and reassessment triggers.

Career should then hand the requirement to the relevant project environment rather than operate the project itself.

Use:

```text
Career question / assessment
→ macro directive + evidence gate
→ project-local planning / learning / implementation / testing
→ project-local evidence preservation
→ return to Career at trigger
→ Career validation / reassessment / next decision
```

Career normally specifies **what must become true and how Career will assess it**. The project specifies **how, where, and in what order to execute it**.

Career must not duplicate or control project-local source-study plans, tests, commands, learning chunks, debugging sequences, implementation plans, TODOs, milestones, or working memory.

## 2. Current workload modes

### Green mode

- Standard focused work: up to 4 hours per production day, Monday through Saturday.
- Standard weekly planning capacity: 24 focused hours.
- Normal daily ceiling: 5 focused hours unless a deliberate exception is safe and useful.
- Work should prioritize the active flagship and approved career-cycle outcomes.

### Yellow mode

- Reduced safe target: approximately 2.5 focused hours.
- Prefer bounded implementation, testing, review, reproduction, evidence organization, or low-risk learning inside the appropriate project environment.
- Avoid consequential architecture, project-allocation, or career-strategy decisions when judgment is materially reduced.
- Missing capacity may be recovered within the same week only when safe; it does not create debt that justifies unsafe overwork.

### Red mode

- No technical hours are required.
- Preserve project and Career continuation.
- Resume later without compensatory overwork.
- A Red day reduces the adjusted weekly target rather than creating a backlog obligation.

Health, safety, legality, privacy, credentials, cost, and platform limits override workload targets.

## 3. Time and output discipline

- Hours are capacity containers, not proof of progress.
- Concrete behavior, evidence, capability, portfolio value, or market learning matters more than time spent.
- Do not pad work to fill a time allocation.
- When a project task finishes early, continue only to the next authorized project responsibility or approved career-cycle outcome.
- Do not begin consequential new work merely to avoid stopping.
- Documentation is justified when it controls a material decision, preserves reusable evidence, enables operation, or communicates a real result better than a simpler mechanism.

## 4. Career pulse and review cadence

### Light Career pulse

A brief weekly or biweekly check may ask:

- Is the primary project still producing meaningful capability and portfolio evidence?
- Is workload sustainable?
- Is ownership growing or is AI dependence increasing?
- Is portfolio or market work being neglected?
- Has a material career decision emerged?

A pulse does not require a repository update unless a material career-level fact or decision changed.

### Formal or event-triggered Career review

A review occurs when:

- a scheduled cycle review arrives;
- Ali requests one;
- a major project gate changes capability or portfolio interpretation;
- a capability or portfolio claim is proposed;
- workload becomes materially unrealistic;
- application readiness is being considered;
- target roles, geography, or work mode may change;
- a new project is proposed;
- the primary project becomes infeasible or misaligned;
- a career cycle ends.

A review records only career-level conclusions in `CAREER_STATE.md`, `tracking/CAPABILITY_EVIDENCE_LEDGER.md`, `portfolio/`, `market/`, or `tracking/CAREER_REVIEW_LOG.md` as applicable.

When a review identifies a project-addressable gap, Career records the gap, required evidence/acceptance condition, and reassessment trigger. Detailed remediation belongs to the relevant project repository/session.

## 5. Project allocation rules

- One primary flagship is the default.
- Supporting work must address a named capability, portfolio, or market gap without competing with the flagship.
- Experimental work requires a bounded question, time ceiling, and stop condition.
- Historical projects remain inactive unless Career assigns a new bounded role.
- A new major project requires evidence that the current portfolio cannot address the intended career need proportionately.
- A project may be paused, narrowed, reclassified, or retired when evidence shows insufficient capability value, portfolio value, feasibility, motivation, or market relevance.

Career assigns project roles and macro evidence requirements. Project repositories control technical execution.

## 6. Anti-diversion commitments

Do not:

- reopen broad project comparison without a real career question and evidence;
- start a competing flagship because the current project becomes difficult;
- convert one blocker into a detached curriculum or new roadmap automatically;
- reactivate Sentinel, AegisLab, or another project merely because work already exists;
- adopt a new career identity from one interesting technology or short exposure;
- force advanced technologies into a project for portfolio appearance;
- treat documentation, repository size, or AI-generated sophistication as capability;
- delay market calibration indefinitely while waiting for perfect readiness;
- create trackers, plans, or templates before the relevant phase becomes active;
- duplicate project live state, execution plans, learning plans, TODOs, or working memory in Career.

## 7. Capability and claim discipline

Career claims require:

- a specific responsibility;
- preserved evidence;
- honest assistance level;
- date and recency;
- confidence;
- transfer limit;
- appropriate changed-case, failure, delayed, or reduced-prompt evidence for stronger claims.

Project completion and capability assessment are separate.

Portfolio and resume claims must distinguish:

- implemented behavior;
- tested behavior;
- observed live behavior;
- experimental or rejected methods;
- AI-generated and AI-assisted work;
- Ali-directed, verified, modified, diagnosed, or owned responsibilities;
- known limitations.

Career may directly test or validate these properties during a formal assessment. Any detailed learning/remediation discovered by the assessment returns to the relevant project/session.

## 8. Career-route change

A material change to working identity, role direction, primary project, major workload, geography, advanced-capability strategy, or market phase requires:

1. the career question;
2. evidence supporting change;
3. the smallest necessary correction;
4. preserved work and claims;
5. affected capability, portfolio, and employability outcomes;
6. reversal or reassessment conditions;
7. an update to the proper canonical files.

Valid triggers include:

- technical or external infeasibility;
- repeated evidence of project-career misalignment;
- sustained workload mismatch;
- market evidence that materially changes target-role feasibility;
- safety, legal, privacy, credential, or cost constraints;
- a completed cycle;
- Ali's explicit formal strategy review.

## 9. Market activation

Career may research representative roles before applications begin.

Applications become active through an explicit readiness decision considering:

- target-role evidence requirements;
- current capability and transfer limits;
- portfolio quality and explanation;
- resume and GitHub positioning;
- practical interview readiness;
- the value of real market feedback.

Readiness does not require mastery of every listed requirement. The decision must identify which gaps are acceptable, which are blocking, and how feedback will update the strategy.

Detailed application, interview, or networking trackers are created only after their phase is activated.

## 10. Advanced-capability exposure

Advanced systems and technologies may be explored when they serve:

- a real project limitation;
- a target-role evidence gap;
- a bounded strategic comparison;
- a credible portfolio need.

No fixed technology quota may force artificial architecture. Exposure, pilot, adoption, and independent capability remain distinct.

Career may authorize the career need, ceiling, and evidence question. The technical project/environment owns the detailed experiment or implementation.

## 11. Document discipline

Before creating or editing a Career artifact, verify:

1. the matter belongs at career level;
2. the file is the canonical owner of that truth;
3. the change is material and durable enough to record;
4. the same fact will not change after an ordinary project session;
5. the artifact is public-safe;
6. a simpler existing file cannot hold the information clearly.

Career plans should contain macro objectives, allocation, evidence gates, claim boundaries, and review triggers. They should not become technical project/session plans.

Use Git history as the default archive. Retain explicit historical files only when they remain useful evidence or orientation.

## 12. Maintenance

Change this contract only when workload modes, Career review behavior, Career-to-project handoff, project allocation rules, anti-diversion commitments, capability claims, market activation, route-change logic, or document discipline changes.

Current cycle dates and outcomes belong in `plans/CURRENT_CAREER_CYCLE.md`; current position belongs in `CAREER_STATE.md`.
