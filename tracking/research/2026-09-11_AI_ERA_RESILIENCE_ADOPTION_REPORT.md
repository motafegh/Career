### P0.1 What the scenario work actually is

- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** Anthropic explicitly presents the model as a structured scenario tool, not a forecast. The three named scenarios receive no probabilities.
- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** occupations are represented as bundles of tasks. Tasks may remain unchanged, be augmented by AI, be automated by AI, or be replaced/expanded by newly created tasks.
- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** the major input dimensions are distinct: AI capability, real-world adoption, autonomy, productivity effect, and worker adjustment/reallocation speed.
- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** this distinction matters because technical capability does not automatically imply immediate labor-market realization; adoption and organizational use are separate modeled variables.
- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** worker adjustment is also separate. Even when the economy grows, slow occupational reallocation can produce meaningful temporary or persistent labor-market pain.

### P0.2 Conditional scenario outputs — not direct labor-market facts

- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** the modest scenario produces effects closer to historically familiar technology diffusion.
- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** the substantial scenario assumes much stronger AI capability in knowledge work, significant autonomy, but incomplete adoption. The model produces faster growth with materially more pressure on knowledge-work employment/wages than in the modest case.
- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** the extreme scenario assumes very strong knowledge-work capability, near-total autonomy for affected work, fast adoption, and essentially no compensating creation of new human knowledge tasks. Under those assumptions the model produces historically extraordinary growth together with severe knowledge-work displacement and a large shift from labor toward capital income.
- **JUDGMENT:** these outputs are useful for stress-testing a strategy against stronger-AI futures, but they are not evidence that Ali's target roles will in fact experience those exact outcomes by 2030.

### P0.3 What drives the alarming result

The severe result does **not** come from one proposition such as `AI gets better`.

It depends on a conjunction closer to:

```text
very high task capability
+ high task autonomy
+ rapid adoption
+ large productivity gains
+ weak/new-task compensation
+ costly/slow worker reallocation
```

**JUDGMENT:** Career should therefore avoid collapsing all AI news into one variable. A frontier-model capability release is not, by itself, equivalent to occupational automation, employer adoption, or loss of human demand.

### P0.4 Explicit limitations and open disagreement

- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** the explorer states that the model is a stark simplification and a work in progress.
- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** omitted or simplified channels include policy response, business cycles, financial-market disruption, aggregate-demand effects, potential catastrophic risks, and sufficiently advanced robotics/physical automation.
- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** the model does not follow heterogeneous individual workers in detail, so displacement costs are represented coarsely.
- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** external reviewers were not asked to endorse the conclusions.
- **SCENARIO/MODEL:** reviewers disagreed on whether highly AI-exposed occupations must shrink at all; some regarded the extreme scenario more as a thought experiment, while others thought the modest scenario might understate already-visible effects.
- **JUDGMENT:** these limitations materially reduce the evidentiary weight of using any one scenario as a direct Career prediction.

### P0.5 Current empirical evidence is materially less extreme

- **EMPIRICAL:** Anthropic's March 2026 labor-market work found that actual observed AI coverage remained well below theoretical technical capability.
- **EMPIRICAL:** that study did not find a systematic unemployment increase for highly exposed workers to date, while it did report suggestive evidence of slower hiring for younger workers in more exposed occupations.
- **EMPIRICAL:** Anthropic's June 2026 Claude Code study found that humans typically made more planning decisions while the agent made more execution decisions.
- **EMPIRICAL:** greater domain expertise was still associated with more successful sessions and greater effective delegation, i.e. persistent returns to expertise in the observed period.
- **JUDGMENT:** current evidence therefore supports taking transition risk seriously without treating technical expertise as already economically obsolete.

### P0.6 Comparison with Career A2/A7

Career A2 already concluded that routine implementation, boilerplate, first-pass code/test/document generation and mechanical changes are becoming cheaper as standalone differentiators.

A2 also already elevated:

- problem/intent ownership;
- system mental models and architecture boundaries;
- verification/evaluation/test design;
- causal diagnosis;
- security/trust/blast-radius control;
- data/evidence quality and provenance;
- cross-system integration;
- operations/accountability;
- domain/product judgment;

- agent/harness engineering.

A7 then converted that into the anchored-hybrid Career model and the nine-part AI-era ownership lens.

**Result:** the new Anthropic scenario work does **not contradict** our current ownership strategy.

It mostly provides a stronger macroeconomic reason to pressure-test whether those responsibilities remain valuable as more execution is automated.

### P0.7 What is genuinely new/useful for our adoption program

The strongest new contributions are not the extreme headline numbers. They are:

1. **Task-bundle framing.** Jobs should be decomposed into responsibilities/tasks instead of treated as indivisible titles.
2. **Capability ≠ adoption ≠ autonomy.** These should remain separate when interpreting AI progress.
3. **Adjustment/reallocation matters.** Even a productive economy can be painful for individuals if workers move slowly between responsibilities/occupations.
4. **New-task creation is a crucial uncertainty.** A strategy should build adaptability rather than assume either permanent existing tasks or guaranteed new roles.
5. **Market contact has option value.** In a rapidly changing task mix, real application/employer feedback helps detect which responsibilities are actually retaining value and access.

### P0.8 Ali-specific consequence

- **ALI/JUDGMENT:** stopping technical learning is not justified by P0.
- **ALI/JUDGMENT:** switching to manual-only coding is also not justified.
- **ALI/JUDGMENT:** the current UpgradePilot direction remains useful because it can develop the very substrate A2 identified: technical mental models, verification, debugging, evidence reasoning, integration, and accountable AI use.
- **ALI/JUDGMENT:** however, learning should be evaluated more explicitly for **transferable responsibility ownership**, not merely accumulation of project-specific implementation knowledge.
- **ALI/JUDGMENT:** Career should continue moving toward real market feedback after E2 rather than using uncertainty about 2030 as a reason to stay in indefinite preparation.

The correct Career response is therefore to pressure-test target roles at the responsibility/task level and strengthen transferable technical judgment, verification, diagnosis, integration and AI leverage while collecting real market feedback—not to abandon technical learning or chase an imagined AI-proof profession.

### P1.2 Technical Implementation / AI Solutions / Automation

### P2.5 Automation-compression heuristic result

The original question was useful but incomplete:

> If a capable AI could implement this entire slice correctly in one minute, what would Ali still need to understand, decide, verify, diagnose, explain, or be accountable for?

P2 found a failure mode: answered badly, this could encourage vague management-only thinking and let technical substrate decay.

The improved form is:

> **If implementation became nearly free, what engineering responsibility would still be mine — and what technical understanding would I still need to verify, diagnose, modify, transfer and defend that responsibility?**

**Disposition entering P6:** strong candidate for **ADOPT NARROWLY / PILOT** in consequential learning slices, not as a mandatory question after every micro-step.

Use when the slice contains a meaningful new behavior, proof boundary, architecture decision, failure mode, integration seam or AI/harness responsibility.

Skip/compress for repetitive/familiar child work.

### P2.6 Transferability heuristic result

Useful form:

```text
What is the transferable engineering responsibility here?
What project-specific/domain knowledge is only needed to support it?
Where else would the same responsibility appear?
```

P2 confirms real transfer for the selected responsibilities:

- synthesis permission/abstention → trustworthy AI/evaluation/decision support;
- target/package evidence separation → data provenance/integration/validation;
- CI static/runtime distinction → software verification/reliability;
- framework deferral → architecture and AI-harness judgment.

**Disposition entering P6:** strong candidate for **ADOPT NARROWLY**, especially in post-implementation learning and portfolio/interview preparation.

Do not force a generic lesson from every file/function.

### P2.7 H4 result — preserve AI leverage, increase responsibility ownership

Strongly supported.

The representative slices show that AI can plausibly make a large share of implementation cheap while leaving meaningful human responsibility in:

- intent/contract;
- evidence admission;
- acceptance criteria;
- proof interpretation;
- changed-case reasoning;

- risk/claim limits;
- communication/accountability.

This does not reduce the need for technical substrate; it changes **why** the substrate matters: not to out-type the agent, but to detect, challenge, verify, modify and diagnose agent-produced systems.

## P3 — Learning-depth and anti-rabbit-hole design

**State:** COMPLETE FOR CURRENT ADOPTION HORIZON

P3 pressure-tested the current Day-90 depth model against P0–P2.

### P3.1 Python / software fundamentals

**Disposition:** **STRENGTHEN, but narrow the definition of strength.**

Depth should emphasize:

- reading/tracing unfamiliar relevant code;
- data structures/types/state;
- function/module/class boundaries;
- contracts/invariants;
- error/failure handling;
- making bounded meaningful changes;
- understanding generated diffs;
- predicting behavior before execution.

Do not optimize for syntax memorization, boilerplate speed or blank-page recreation of large systems.

### P3.2 Testing / verification / evaluation

**Disposition:** **STRENGTHEN materially.**

This is one of the most resilient and market-supported responsibilities.

Depth should include:

- what a test/eval actually proves;
- oracle/acceptance-criteria quality;
- changed/negative/adversarial cases;
- source bug vs fixture bug vs environment/provider failure;
- focused proof vs system proof;
- false confidence from green checks;
- evaluation of model/agent output as well as ordinary software behavior.

### P3.3 Debugging / causal diagnosis

**Disposition:** **STRENGTHEN materially.**

AI can generate more candidate fixes faster; that makes causal localization more important, not less.

Target behavior remains:

```text
symptom
→ plausible cause families
→ discriminating evidence
→ failing boundary
→ repair choice
→ verification
```

Do not manufacture failures for evidence.

### P3.4 Code reading + meaningful modification

**Disposition:** **STRENGTHEN.**

Source reading alone is insufficient; manual typing volume is also insufficient.

The high-value combination is:

```text
owned mental model
+ meaningful change/correction
+ verification
+ explanation of effects/limits
```

### P3.5 System/data/evidence reasoning

**Disposition:** **RETAIN as a current strength and connect more tightly to executable implementation.**

Ali should continue strong work on provenance, uncertainty, identity, state and authority, but avoid becoming someone who can discuss evidence perfectly while remaining unable to modify/debug the system that carries it.

### P3.6 Intent/specification/acceptance ownership

**Disposition:** **ADD AS AN EXPLICIT CROSS-CUTTING RESPONSIBILITY, not a new curriculum.**

This was already implicit in A2 but deserves stronger visibility under automation pressure.

For substantive responsibilities Ali should increasingly be able to state before implementation:

- what success/failure/non-goal means;
- what evidence would justify acceptance;
- what stronger claim is intentionally not earned;
- what changed case should discriminate the design.

This is not product-management-only work; it must remain tied to technical proof.

### P3.7 AI-agent / harness engineering

**Disposition:** **STRENGTHEN as a differentiator, with a hard anti-prompting boundary.**

Keep:

- context/instruction design;
- task decomposition;
- model/tool selection;
- permission boundaries;
- memory/state control;
- eval/test feedback loops;
- agent drift/overengineering detection;
- human checkpoints;
- repository/tooling legibility.

Require enough technical ownership to verify the result. `I can prompt an agent` is not a Career differentiator by itself.

### P3.8 APIs / HTTP / external systems

**Disposition:** **RETAIN solid working depth; strengthen when real integration work appears.**

Focus on request/response contracts, identity, authentication concepts, failure states, timeouts/retries where applicable, provider boundaries and diagnosis rather than memorizing provider APIs.

### P3.9 Git / review

**Disposition:** **STRENGTHEN proportionately.**

AI increases change volume, so diff/review/change-control skill becomes more valuable. Keep the existing goal for real branch/commit/PR/review/conflict evidence rather than synthetic ceremony.

### P3.10 Linux / operations / observability

**Disposition:** **RETAIN working depth; strengthen opportunistically through real failures/operation.**

The important value is being able to inspect systems and failures, not command memorization.

### P3.11 SQL / relational

**Disposition:** **RETAIN practical working-depth target and execute the already-authorized bounded intervention if no natural route appears.**
